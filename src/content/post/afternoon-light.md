Most rooms look their best for about forty minutes a day. In a west-facing room in autumn, that window opens at around four o'clock, when the sun drops low enough to come straight through the glass and lay a bright, sharp shape across the far wall.

## Wait for the wall

Before you move anything, watch the light. Sit in the room for an hour with the camera on the floor beside you. The shape of the window will slide across the floor, climb the wall and soften as the sun goes down. Pick the moment you like, then come back the next day and be ready for it.

## Remove one thing

A room that photographs well usually has one object fewer than you think. Take out the second chair, the extra cushion, the plant that nearly fits. Shadow needs empty space to read as a shape.

## Expose for the bright patch

Meter on the brightest part of the wall and let everything else fall into shadow. Plaster, timber and linen keep their texture in the dark much better than you expect, and the picture keeps its calm.
