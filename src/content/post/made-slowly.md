The kiln is fired twice a month. Everything else in the workshop follows from that: when the clay is wedged, when the bowls are trimmed, when the glaze buckets are stirred. There is no rush, because there is no way to make the kiln go faster.

## The shelf

Between throwing and firing, every piece waits on a long timber shelf by the window. Some wait three days, some wait two weeks. The rule is simple: a bowl leaves the shelf when it is bone dry and when one of the two potters has picked it up and decided it is worth the space in the kiln.

## Three glazes

The workshop uses three glazes and nothing else: an oat white, a rust iron and a deep bottle green. Limiting the palette means that every piece belongs with every other piece. A customer can buy one bowl this year and one next year, and they will sit together on the same table.

## Buying well

If you want to buy handmade work, ask when the next firing is and wait for it. The best pieces rarely sit on the shop table for long.
