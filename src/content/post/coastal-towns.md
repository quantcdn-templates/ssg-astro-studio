Walk up from the harbour at two in the afternoon and the town goes silent. The shutters are closed, the lanes are in shade, and the only sound is water moving against the sea wall. Nothing about this is an accident. Every street you climb was laid out by people who had no air conditioning and a very clear idea of where the sun would be.

## Walls that hold the night

The houses here are built from rubble stone, often sixty centimetres thick, then rendered in lime and washed in white or pale ochre. The mass of the wall soaks up the heat of the day and lets it go slowly after dark. Inside, the air stays close to the temperature of the previous night well into the afternoon.

The pale finish does a second job. Fresh limewash reflects most of the sunlight that hits it, so the surface stays cool enough to touch at midday. It also has to be renewed. In most of these towns, families repaint before the first festival of the year, and the whole hillside turns bright again in a single week.

> A good coastal house is not designed against the weather. It is designed with it, and then repainted every spring.

## Lanes that bend on purpose

From the water, the lanes look random. They are not. Most of them turn every thirty or forty metres, which breaks the wind that comes off the sea in winter and keeps at least one side of the street in shade in summer. Steps are shallow and wide so that a person carrying a load can climb them without stopping.

Openings are small and placed low on the windward side, larger and higher on the lee. A single courtyard, sometimes only three metres across, pulls cool air through the house as the warm air rises out of it.

## What we can borrow

None of this is exotic. Thermal mass, cross-ventilation, shade and reflective surfaces are taught in every architecture school. What the old towns add is patience. They were built and maintained by people who expected to live in them for fifty years, and every decision shows it.

- **Build heavy where you can.** Mass evens out the day.
- **Shade the street, not only the house.** Cool lanes make cool rooms.
- **Plan for upkeep.** A finish you renew every year is still a good finish.

The next time you visit one of these towns, take the long way up from the harbour. Notice where the wind stops and where the shade falls. The buildings will tell you how they work, if you walk slowly enough to listen.
